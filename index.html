How to read the nine service areas

The breadth is useful when a ranking problem crosses channels. Local visibility may need suburb pages and review signals, while a store may need category structure and product copy, and a service firm may need cleaner crawl paths and intent mapped pages.

Treat each named area as a question rather than a package. For local work, ask which suburbs have enough demand to justify dedicated pages. For online retail, ask how organic visits will reach commercial pages, not only articles. For technical work, ask which fixes are structural and which are cosmetic. A short benchmark helps: if a proposal cannot connect an activity to indexation, relevance, authority or user experience, it is harder to evaluate against the stated price range.

Pricing and timeline signals to screen

The published figures are screening tools, not promises. Monthly retainers are described as typically $1,200 to $4,000 plus GST, varying with competition, scope and target keywords. Project audits are described as starting from around $1,500.

Timeframes matter because SEO compounds unevenly. The source guidance says measurable movement for mid competition keywords is often seen across three to six months, while law, finance or national retail may require nine to twelve months. Any forecast should therefore be read against sector difficulty, current site health and the amount of content already present.

  • Cheap plans that skip diagnosis usually shift risk to later months.
  • Expensive plans should still show work completed, not only access to tools.
  • Fast guarantees sit poorly beside a model where rankings are controlled by an external algorithm.

Reporting you can audit

The clearest filter is a report tied to tasks. A monthly update should name what was fixed, what was published, which links or mentions were sought, what moved in ranking, and what is planned next. Traffic graphs alone can hide weak execution.

Ask for wording that a non specialist can check. If a report says improved technical health, it should point to pages indexed, layout stability, internal links changed or structured data corrected. If it says content, it should show the keyword mapped, the page served and the action users are expected to take.

This standard is consistent with general consumer expectations around clear claims and substantiation, while web accessibility and markup quality connect to public guidance from standards bodies rather than sales language.

Who should pause before buying

Some businesses are not ready for a retainer yet. A brand new site with thin pages may need information architecture first. A firm with no review process may need local operations fixed before local rankings can hold. A store with duplicated supplier copy may need product differentiation before link outreach makes sense.

Readiness signs include a live site, clear services, stable contact details, enough margin to fund several months, and a decision maker who can approve changes. The strongest brief is boring and concrete: current blockers, target suburbs or categories, budget ceiling, approval path and the metric that matters, such as calls, bookings or orders.

  1. Collect the baseline. Gather current rankings, indexed pages, main competitors, service suburbs and the enquiry or revenue metric that matters.
  2. Request the written audit. Seek findings on crawl, indexation, speed, internal links, backlinks and competitor gaps before discussing a retainer.
  3. Compare scope and sequence. Check which fixes come first and whether content, local signals or links are delayed until the base is cleaner.
  4. Set review points. Agree what will be reported monthly and when a decision to continue, change course or stop will be made.

Common questions

Is a free audit enough to judge a provider? It is a useful sample, not proof. The value lies in whether the findings are specific, prioritised and explained in plain language. A generic checklist with no reference to indexation, speed, internal links, backlinks or competitors is weaker evidence than a short list that names the likely blockers.

Why do quoted timelines vary so much? Because difficulty differs by sector, site condition and keyword intent. The supplied guidance frames mid competition movement around three to six months and tougher law, finance or national retail terms around nine to twelve months. Those are planning ranges, not guarantees.

What should a buyer refuse to accept? Be wary of guaranteed first page positions, reports that avoid completed tasks, and scopes that cannot say which problems are urgent. Since rankings are controlled by an external algorithm, certainty claims deserve scrutiny rather than trust.
